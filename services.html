Services

Three Pathways

Three pathways, one shared kaupapa.

Personal and whānau support, professional learning, and collective experiences, each creating opportunities for connection, understanding, and growth, guided by the needs, strengths, and aspirations of those who walk alongside us.

01

Individual and Personal Support

For individuals, whānau, and small groups seeking healing, reconnection, or support.

Individual

One-to-One Sessions

Individual sessions alongside ngā hōiho, held within the Medicine Circle. An opportunity to slow down, deepen awareness, explore personal experiences, and reconnect with yourself through relationship with the herd, the whenua, and the wisdom that already exists within you.

Whānau

Whānau and Group Sessions

Shared experiences for whānau and small groups, creating opportunities to strengthen relationships, deepen understanding, and foster meaningful connection. Through time alongside the horses, participants can gain new perspectives on themselves, one another, and the patterns that shape their lives.

Constellation

Whānau Constellations

Systemic whānau constellation work alongside ngā hōiho, exploring the relationships, patterns, and connections that shape our lives. These sessions offer a gentle space to deepen awareness of family and relational dynamics, honour what has come before, and explore possibilities for greater belonging, balance, and right relationship.

02

Professional & Practitioner Pathways

For those working in helping, leadership, and relational fields, offering opportunities to deepen understanding and practice through embodied learning, reflection, and meaningful connection.

One-to-One

One-to-One Mentoring

Individual mentoring for practitioners, leaders, and those working alongside people and whānau. These sessions offer space to reflect on practice, deepen relational understanding, explore embodied awareness, and integrate culturally grounded perspectives into everyday work.

Development

Training & Professional Development

Workshops, wānanga, and professional learning experiences exploring trauma-informed practice, Te Ao Māori, relational approaches, and the wisdom of the horse–human relationship. Bringing together theory, lived experience, and embodied learning, these opportunities support practitioners to broaden their understanding, confidence, and capability.

Partnership

Referrer & Agency Partnerships

We work alongside referring agencies, GPs, mental health professionals, community organisations, and support services to establish thoughtful, collaborative pathways of support. Through open communication and shared understanding, we aim to provide continuity of care that honours each person's circumstances, dignity, and needs.

03

Community & Collective Learning

For organisations, teams, communities, and groups seeking to strengthen relationships, deepen understanding, and explore new possibilities for collective wellbeing.

Community

Community Wānanga & Workshops

Gatherings that weave together the wisdom of Te Ao Māori, ngā hōiho, whenua, and community. Through shared learning, reflection, and meaningful connection, participants are invited to deepen understanding, nurture belonging, and contribute to the wellbeing of people, place, and future generations.

Whakarongo

Deep Listening and the Wisdom of Presence

A principle that guides our work is whakarongo. While often translated simply as “listening,” within Te Ao Māori its meaning is much deeper. It is an invitation to listen not only with the ears, but with the whole self, through the body, the senses, the heart, and the spirit.

At H.E.A.R.T.S., we understand listening as more than hearing words. It is paying attention to what is felt, sensed, and observed within ourselves, within others, and within the wider environment. It is listening to the stories carried in the body, the wisdom held within relationships, and the subtle communications that arise through connection with ngā hōiho and the whenua.

This understanding aligns with both trauma-informed practice and phenomenology, where curiosity, presence, and observation are valued more highly than assumptions or predetermined answers. Rather than rushing to diagnose, explain, or interpret, we create conditions where people can discover their own meaning and understanding.

The Wisdom of Deep Hanging Out

In cultural anthropology, there is a recognised methodology known as Deep Hanging Out. It describes the intentional practice of slowing down, setting aside agendas, and spending time in genuine relationship with people and place. Rather than trying to control the experience, the practitioner becomes present enough for insight and understanding to emerge naturally.

As horse people, this is something we have long understood.

What may appear from the outside as “just sitting in the paddock” is often one of the most important parts of the work. There is no task to complete, no performance required, and nowhere to be other than where we are. We simply spend time in relationship with the herd.

When we let go of the need to direct, fix, or achieve, something begins to shift. The horses soften. The nervous system slows. Awareness deepens. What was previously hidden often becomes visible.

In these moments, healing is not something being done to a person. Rather, it emerges through relationship, presence, and connection. The horses invite us into a different way of being, one that is grounded in authenticity, reciprocity, and trust.

This is why we place such value on slowing down. In a world that constantly asks us to do more, move faster, and think harder, spending time with the herd teaches us another possibility. Sometimes the most meaningful insights arise not through striving, but through simply being present enough to notice what is already there.

Our Foundational Framework

From a Mātauranga Māori Lens

Mātauranga Māori offers a deeply relational understanding of life, recognising that people are not separate from their whakapapa, their environment, or the relationships that shape their world. These understandings are foundational to our practice, informing how we approach connection, healing, and the restoration of mauri across all areas of our work, including:

  • •Whānau Constellations and systemic exploration.
  • •Equine-facilitated relational work alongside the herd.
  • •Trauma-informed practice and somatic safety.
  • •Connection with whenua and Te Taiao.

Whakapapa

Whakapapa connects us to those who came before us, those around us, and those yet to come. It encompasses our relationships with people, whenua, and the natural world. The experiences, stories, strengths, and challenges carried through generations can continue to shape how we experience life and relate to others. Acknowledging these connections can open pathways to greater understanding, reconnection, and healing.

Wairua

Wairua is an integral dimension of our being and our relationship with the world around us. It acknowledges the connections that extend beyond what can be seen or expressed in words. It invites us to listen deeply, to recognise what is felt and sensed, and to honour the spiritual dimensions of our relationships with ourselves, others, our ancestors, and the natural world.

A Place of Belonging

Belonging is fundamental to our sense of connection and wellbeing. Within a relational understanding of healing, there is space to acknowledge those who have been forgotten, excluded, or disconnected. When people, experiences, and relationships are recognised and welcomed, opportunities emerge for reconnection, restored dignity, and a renewed sense of belonging.

An Important Distinction: Mātauranga Māori is not a single, fixed framework. Understandings and expressions differ across iwi, hapū, and whānau. The wording above is intended as a broad introduction, not a definitive or exhaustive account of Māori knowledge.
